SYSLAB is a full-scale energy system at Risø that you configure yourself — busbar by busbar, unit by unit — and then control through an open API. Real turbines, real batteries, real heat, real faults.

Three things make it different from a simulation.

You choose the topology

279 remotely operated breakers mean the grid is not fixed. Put a feeder together in the morning, create an overvoltage condition on purpose, and take it apart in the afternoon. Six sites, two busbars each, 8 km of real cable between them — so line impedance and voltage drop are physical, not parameters.

Electricity and heat, one lab

A 4.5 km district heating network runs across the same site as the electrical grid, with a CHP unit, dump loads, storage and heat pumps on shared instrumentation. Sector coupling is something you measure here rather than assume.

Control is yours to deploy

Every unit is reachable through one API, and controllers can run centrally or on the units themselves. Peer-to-peer, cell-based and hierarchical architectures all run on the same hardware. Long historic time series come with it, for training data-driven controllers.
